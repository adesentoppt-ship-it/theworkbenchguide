Before you build a shed: 5 things to check

  1. Permits and setbacks. Many US towns require a permit above a certain size and set minimum distances from property lines. Call your local building department first.
  2. HOA rules. If you have a homeowners association, check its rules on size, color and placement.
  3. Foundation. A level gravel pad, concrete blocks or a slab will decide how long your shed lasts. Plan it before the walls.
  4. Size for the real use. Measure the mower, bikes or tools you want to store, then add room to move.
  5. Total budget. Price lumber, roofing, hardware, paint and tool rental. Compare it with a kit or a pre-built shed.

Who should buy it – and who shouldn't

Good fit: you're comfortable with basic carpentry, you want to compare designs, and you'd rather build than buy pre-made.

Not a good fit: you have no time or tools for a multi-day project, or you need engineer-stamped drawings for a permit.

Alternatives

  • Pre-cut shed kits from home improvement stores – faster, but less flexible and often more expensive.
  • Pre-built sheds delivered to your yard – no building, highest cost.
  • A single paid plan from an independent designer – good if you already know the size and style you want.

Verdict

If you want to build your own shed and like having many designs to choose from, My Shed Plans is worth a look, especially with the refund window. Check your local permit rules first, and be realistic about the time a first build takes.
